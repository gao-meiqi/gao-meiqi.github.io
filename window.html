I'm sitting inside the Guangdong Museum of Art. Across from me hangs the enormous backdrop of the exhibitions Qingzhou Smile and Dunhuang in a Single Thought, painted in shades of turquoise and vermilion.
Yico's Machine is playing through my headphones.
One thing I know for certain is that beauty exists objectively. It is simply there, waiting to be perceived, quietly attracting those who remain sensitive enough to notice it.

There is always a notebook and my laptop in my bag. The notebook has been with me since childhood, while the laptop has become a necessity because of lattice QCD. Today, I also packed a pair of swimming goggles and a towel. I'm planning to go to Yuexiu Swimming Pool this afternoon.
When I came out of the subway, it was raining heavily.
While flipping through one of my old notebooks in the museum, I found something I had written in the winter of 2023. I realized that the feeling I often have — "It's so beautiful. I want to remember it." — resonates deeply with another sentence that has stayed with me for years: "This idea is beautiful. Of course it should be published."

Those words came from Yang Chen-Ning's Dawn, where he reflected on his years as a student. I first read them during my undergraduate years, before I had even started studying physics.
Perhaps these two thoughts are closer than I once imagined. One is about preserving beauty. The other is about sharing beautiful ideas.
I wonder if I could bring my research and my life a little closer together. Physics, music, exhibitions, weather, photography... Maybe they are all different ways of observing the same world.
Or perhaps they should remain separate.
