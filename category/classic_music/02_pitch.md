# 02. Why Does Music Have Pitch?

> *Before learning notes, I want to understand why notes exist.*

---

## A Question

When I clap my hands,

```
clap   clap   clap
```

I can clearly hear a rhythm.

But I cannot sing it.

Why?

---

Now imagine a violin plays a long sound.

Suddenly, I can tell whether it is high or low.

Something new has appeared.

We call it **pitch**.

---

## From Physics to Music

In physics, sound is a vibration.

A vibration has a frequency.

For example,

* 220 Hz
* 440 Hz
* 880 Hz

Nature only knows frequencies.

It does not know "Do", "Re", or "Mi".

These names were invented by humans.

Music begins when we start organizing frequencies.

Just as geometry organizes space,

music organizes sound.

---

## What Is Pitch?

Pitch is **how our ears perceive frequency**.

Frequency belongs to physics.

Pitch belongs to perception.

This distinction feels surprisingly important.

It reminds me that music is not only about nature,

but also about how humans experience nature.

> humans experience nature

---

## The First Mystery

If frequency is continuous,

why do we choose only a few pitches?

Why not infinitely many?

Even more strangely,

why do notes separated by a factor of two

feel somehow "the same"?

For example,

220 Hz

↓

440 Hz

↓

880 Hz

To physics, they are different frequencies.

To music,

they are the same note,

just in different octaves(八度). 

> Why?

I do not know the full answer yet.

But I know this question will eventually lead to scales,

keys,

and harmony.

---

## A Thought

Mathematics discovers patterns.

Physics discovers laws.

Perhaps music discovers relationships.

---

## Listening

Try listening to a single sustained note(音符).

Do not think about melody.

Ask only one question:

> Can I feel "high" and "low" before I know any music theory?
