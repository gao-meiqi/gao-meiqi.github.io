# 03. Notes

> *Humans gave names to sounds long before they explained them.*

---

## Why Do We Name Notes?

Imagine trying to describe a melody without names.

"Play the higher sound."

"No, a little lower."

"Not that one."

Very quickly,

we realize that sound needs a language.

Notes are that language.

---

## Two Naming Systems

Today, there are two common systems.

### Letter Names

```
C  D  E  F  G  A  B
```

These letters are widely used in English-speaking countries and in music theory.

---

### Solfège

```
Do  Re  Mi  Fa  Sol  La  Si
```

This system emphasizes singing and hearing relationships between notes.

Neither system is more "correct".

They are simply two ways of naming the same musical world.

---

## Where Did They Come From?

The letter names have very old roots in medieval Europe.

The syllables

Do Re Mi Fa Sol La Si

came from a medieval hymn,

where each phrase began one step higher than the previous one.

What began as a teaching method

eventually became one of the most universal languages in music.

I find it beautiful that

music itself helped create the language used to teach music.

---

## Why Does C Come First?

It doesn't.

Historically,

the musical alphabet begins with **A**.

The piano only makes it look as if C is the beginning,

because C major happens to use only the white keys.

This is one of those details that are easy to misunderstand when starting.

---

## Notes Are Not Music

Knowing note names

is like knowing the alphabet.

No one becomes a writer

simply by memorizing letters.

Likewise,

music begins not with notes,

but with relationships between notes.

---

## Listening

Listen to the opening of Bach,

Beethoven,

or Tchaikovsky.

Do not try to identify notes.

Instead,

ask yourself:

> Can I hear that some notes feel like "home", while others feel like they want to go somewhere else?

Perhaps this feeling is more fundamental than the names themselves.
